The visual system
1. Core idea

Auric Editorial Commerce means the site behaves like a luxury magazine crossed with a private concierge desk.

The feeling should be:
curated, selective, warm, and assured.

Not:
cheap, crowded, salesy, or frantic.

2. Visual principles

Curated over crowded.
Only show a few offers at a time. Every card should feel selected.

Trust before temptation.
Use policy snippets, partner credibility, and clear savings math.

Luxury through space.
Empty space is not wasted space here. It is a status signal.

Editorial hierarchy.
The page should read like a feature story, not a marketplace grid.

Soft persuasion.
The design should invite. It should not push.

3. Materials

Use a material language that feels tactile and expensive.

Think:
ivory paper, brushed brass, matte stone, warm linen, soft shadow, and photographic glass.

Avoid:
heavy neon, loud gradients, chrome overload, and anything that feels like a gaming UI.

The current rainbow mark can live on social media and in the brand system as an accent symbol, but the main website should use a refined monochrome or gold wordmark.

4. Lighting

Use soft natural daylight.

Highlights should be gentle, not glossy.
Shadows should be shallow, not dramatic.
Edges should feel softly lit, like a high end hospitality brochure or a premium fashion catalog.

5. Texture

Use barely visible paper grain in backgrounds.
Use satin metal only for accents.
Use blur only in background layers, never as the main content state.

The site should feel physical, but not fake physical.

6. Typography

Use a high contrast editorial serif for headlines and a clean grotesk for everything functional.

Headline role:
elegance, authority, aspiration.

Body role:
clarity, trust, speed.

UI labels:
small uppercase with generous letter spacing.

Pricing:
tabular numerals, sharp alignment, no clutter.

The typography should make the brand feel like it has taste, patience, and money.

7. Color palette

Use a very narrow palette.

Primary:
ivory, warm white, champagne gold, charcoal.

Secondary:
stone, taupe, muted rose, soft olive.

Accent:
one deep jewel tone only, such as forest green or burgundy, used sparingly for seasonal campaigns or premium partner highlights.

Suggested palette direction:

Ivory, background base
Warm White, surface base
Champagne Gold, accent and CTA
Charcoal, text and structure
Stone, borders and panels
Taupe, secondary UI
Muted Rose, emotional warmth
Deep Olive, premium nature accent


8. Iconography

Use thin line icons with rounded ends.
Keep the stroke weight consistent.
Avoid filled icons except where absolutely necessary.
Icons should feel like hotel signage or editorial symbols, not app store stickers.

9. Motion

Motion should be slow, confident, and barely noticeable.

Rules:
entry animations under 250 ms,
soft parallax for photography,
gentle card lift on hover,
no aggressive bounce,
no flashing,
no overdone scroll theatrics.

The motion language should feel like a concierge opening a door, not a slot machine.

10. Depth

Use shallow depth.

Layering should come from:
spacing,
thin borders,
soft shadows,
slight elevation,
and overlapping image cards.

Avoid heavy 3D. Avoid glass everywhere. Avoid fake skeuomorphic depth.

11. UI components

The key components should be:

Hero section with one emotional headline and one proof driven subhead.
Trust strip with partner count, savings, and response assurance.
Offer cards with original price, deal price, validity, and location.
Category tiles for travel, dining, wellness, entertainment, stays.
Partner story blocks with venue imagery.
FAQ accordion for trust and conditions.
Sticky filter bar with location, category, price range, and date.
Testimonial cards that feel like quotes, not reviews.
A final CTA block that feels exclusive, not desperate.

What the homepage should communicate

The page should say:

We curate quality deals.
We work with real partners.
Savings are real and easy to understand.
The experience feels premium, not bargain bin.
Booking is simple and trustworthy.

That is the real psychological job of the design.

What to avoid

Do not make it look like:
a coupon directory,
a loud promo flyer,
a cheap travel portal,
or a generic AI generated landing page.

Do not use:
too many gradients,
too many colors,
too many outlines,
too many badges,
or too many stacked cards.
